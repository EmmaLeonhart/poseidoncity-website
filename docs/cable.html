The Pitcairn Cable

The first undersea internet route between South America and the Asia-Pacific is being built across the South Pacific. Pitcairn sits right beside it.

A gap in the world's internet

Almost all of the world's internet traffic travels through undersea fibre-optic cables. South America's Pacific coast is well connected to the north: Google's Curie cable, for example, has run from Los Angeles to Valparaíso, Chile, since 2019. But not one cable crosses the South Pacific between South America and Asia or Oceania. Traffic between Chile and Australia today goes the long way round, through North America.

That is about to change. Humboldt, a joint venture of Google and the Chilean government's Desarrollo País, with French Polynesia's telecom operator OPT, will run about 14,800 km from Valparaíso to Sydney by way of Tahiti. It is the first cable route ever planned between South America and the Asia-Pacific, and it is expected to enter service in 2027.

Pitcairn is on the way

Between Tahiti and Chile lie more than 7,800 km of open ocean. Pitcairn lies about 2,200 km from Tahiti and about 5,700 km from Valparaíso, and only about 140 km from the direct line between them.

Cables reach islands near their path with a branch: a short spur from a branching unit on the main line. That is how Tahiti joins the new route. A branch to Pitcairn would give the island fibre to two continents at once, Australia and Asia one way, South America the other.

It has been done for islands this small

Saint Helena, another British overseas territory, with about 4,500 people, was connected in 2023 by a branch from Google's Equiano cable along the coast of Africa. The branch was paid for by a €21.5 million EU grant. Latency to London fell from about 657 ms on satellite to about 131 ms.

Niue and the Cook Islands, the two states in free association with New Zealand that Pitcairn hopes to join one day, moved off satellite in 2020 with the Manatua cable, a regional system shared with Samoa and French Polynesia.

The plan

  1. A Pitcairn data centre. Today the island reaches the internet through Starlink. The first step is a data centre on the island, fibre-linked to the homes and businesses of Adamstown, so the island already has one hub that a cable can land at.
  2. A landing on the South Pacific route. Work with the Humboldt partners and other cable builders on a Pitcairn branch, and make the case for the island as a landing point.
  3. A real place on the map of the internet. With fibre to South America and the Asia-Pacific, Pitcairn can host data and computing under its own laws, and serve as a stop on future cables across the South Pacific.

A cable landing turns Pitcairn from the most remote community in the Pacific into a node between two continents. It is the foundation for the technology haven Project Poseidon is building.
